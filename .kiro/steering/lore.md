# Lore bible

All names of people and places below are **fictional** unless marked as a traditional figure. The game is *inspired by* Javanese wayang and ruwatan traditions; it does not claim to depict a real ritual accurately. Keep that framing in the credits.

## Setting
- **Karangsepi** — fictional village in Central Java. Rice fields, a river, one road.
- **The joglo house** of the Wicitro family, with its traditional areas:
  - **Pendopo** — open front pavilion where performances happen. Holds the **kelir** (screen), the empty hook where the **blencong** (oil lamp) normally hangs, the **kotak** (puppet chest) and **debog** (banana trunk the puppets are planted in). Hub and ending.
  - **Pringgitan** — passage between pendopo and the house. Tutorial area.
  - **Dalem** — main family room. Old photos, grandfather's chair.
  - **Senthong** — three back rooms: *kiwa* (left), *tengen* (right), *tengah* (centre, locked — climax).
  - **Gandok** — side pavilion where the gamelan musicians slept.
  - **Pawon** — kitchen. Oil source. Most dangerous area.

## Characters
- **The player** — the grandchild, unnamed, raised abroad, returned for the funeral. Speaks to the player through short journal lines only.
- **Ki Harjo Wicitro** (fictional, 1931–2026) — the dalang. Voice appears only through his lontar notes.
- **Batara Kala** (traditional figure) — the devourer. In KELIR, a presence that exists only in shadow. Never fully seen; only silhouette, knocking, and the cold.

## The five puppets (one act each)
| # | Puppet | Found in | Act meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Semar** (traditional) | Pringgitan | The guardian clown-sage opens the story and teaches the player to read the light |
| 2 | **Batara Guru** (traditional) | Dalem | The king of the gods; how Kala came into being |
| 3 | **Dewi Uma** (traditional) | Gandok | Kala's mother; grief and what was left unfinished |
| 4 | **Batara Kala** (traditional) | Pawon | Holding Kala's own puppet angers him — the hunt intensifies |
| 5 | **Dalang Kandhabuwana** (traditional figure in Murwakala tellings) | Senthong tengah (unlocks after 4 puppets) | The divine puppeteer who tamed Kala through performance |

Finale: **tancep kayon** — planting the gunungan in the centre of the debog, which traditionally marks the end of a performance. Dawn breaks.

## Cultural-respect rules
1. **The ritual is never evil.** The horror is that it was left *unfinished*. Ruwatan is still practised; treat it with respect.
2. Traditional figures (Semar, Batara Guru, Dewi Uma, Batara Kala) are depicted with dignity, never mocked.
3. Never imply Javanese people, Islam, Kejawen or any living practice is sinister.
4. Puppet silhouettes are **original designs inspired by** wayang conventions, not copies of a specific historical puppet.
5. No real dalang's name, voice, or recording is used.
6. Aksara Jawa is decorative and always translated; never put fake or garbled aksara in a place it claims to say something.
7. Before shipping, flag any lore line you are unsure about with `// LORE-CHECK` so a human can verify it.

## Tone
Quiet, sad, then terrifying. Dread over gore. No blood, no body horror. The scariest moment is a silhouette that should not be there.

## Verification notes (spec review, 13 Nov 2026)
Checked against published sources; a Javanese reader should still confirm anything marked LORE-CHECK.
- **The carried blencong.** Traditionally the blencong hangs above the dalang and is not carried. One journal line explains it: the player takes down grandfather's blencong from its hook to light the way.
- **Dewi Uma.** Tellings of Kala's birth differ. In a common one Kala is born from Batara Guru's *kama salah* (misdirected seed) falling into the ocean. Some retellings involve Guru forcing himself on Uma. Act 3 stays with Uma's grief and the curse, and **never depicts or implies coercion**. "Kala's mother" wording is LORE-CHECK.
- **Kandhabuwana** is Batara Wisnu disguised as a dalang in the Murwakala ruwatan; he reads the Rajah Kalacakra written on Kala's body. The game may refer to the Rajah Kalacakra but must **never write out its text**: it is still used as a protective formula.
- **Semar opening the story** is this game's framing, not Murwakala's (Semar usually enters in the goro-goro, mid-performance). Present it as the grandfather's own telling.
- **Senthong tengah** is traditionally the most sacred room of the house (associated with Dewi Sri). It is sealed because it is sacred, never because it is Kala's lair.
- **Ki Harjo Wicitro**: no real dalang of that name found; still confirm with an Indonesian reader before shipping.
- **Aksara Jawa**: keep to 2–3 strings, each with a checked transliteration and a `// LORE-CHECK` until verified.
